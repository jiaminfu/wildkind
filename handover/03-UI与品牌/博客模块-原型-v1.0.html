Why PVD is not plating

2 November 2026 · 2 min read
Cover 16:9

Plating sits on top of the metal and flakes off it. Physical vapour deposition bonds at the molecular level, so the gold layer flexes with the steel underneath. That single difference is why a $76 necklace can survive a year of salt water.

What is actually happening in the chamber

The piece goes into a vacuum chamber. A target metal is vaporised, and the vapour condenses onto the surface as a layer roughly 0.3 microns thick — about one three-hundredth of a human hair. Because the layer is formed atom by atom rather than glued on, there is no boundary for water to get under.

The colour is not a coating. It is the surface. Studio notes, March

What this means day to day

  • Pool, sea, shower, gym — the layer does not care.
  • Rinse in fresh water afterwards and dry with a soft cloth.
  • Chlorine dulls the shine before it does anything to the colour.

Where plating still wins

Cost, and only cost. A plated piece can be made for a fifth of a PVD piece at the same shape — which is why the $12 earrings on a marketplace page exist. They will look identical in the photograph. They will not look identical in fourteen months.

If the colour does change, we replace the piece. Free, for life, no receipt needed — send a photo to hello@lastgrove.com. That is the entire warranty.

The piece this article is about — 18k gold PVD over 316L, $76.
